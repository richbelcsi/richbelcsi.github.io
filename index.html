01 / Overview

Description

Our survey paper examines agent-based modeling and simulation of urban traffic using New York City as a motivating case study. Agent-based models represent individual vehicles or travelers as decision-making entities whose interactions contribute to network-wide traffic patterns. We will investigate how researchers model route selection, differences in driver behavior, and responses to changing road conditions to explain the emergence and persistence of traffic congestion.

Our guiding question is how these models evaluate strategies for reducing congestion while balancing behavioral realism, computational efficiency, and agreement with observed traffic data. We will compare approaches according to their agent representations, behavioral assumptions, interaction mechanisms, simulation scale, and data requirements. Particular attention will be given to calibration and validation: how researchers select model parameters and evaluate whether simulated traffic reproduces observed patterns, such as travel times, traffic volumes, and queue lengths. This comparison will help assess the evidence supporting each approach and the limitations affecting its conclusions.

Although New York City motivates the survey paper, we will include studies from other cities when their methods offer relevant insights. Applications such as traffic signal coordination, route guidance, lane management, and travel incentives will provide context for evaluating model capabilities. We will also consider how surveyed approaches could inform a future DEVSJAVA implementation, particularly the representation of individual behavior within interacting vehicle, road, and intersection models.

Through this review, we aim to organize the literature into meaningful categories, synthesize major findings, and identify unresolved challenges and opportunities for future research. The bibliography currently contains 60 publications, with 20 selected for detailed reading. Preliminary summaries and annotations describe each paper’s contribution, relevant limitations, and connection to investigating urban traffic congestion.
